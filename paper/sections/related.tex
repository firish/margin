\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{LLMs for qualitative and content coding.} LLMs have been used to apply codebooks deductively
\citep{xiao2023supporting,chew2023llm}, to replace or assist crowd annotation \citep{gilardi2023chatgpt,ziems2024can}, to
code open-ended survey responses \citep{mellon2024ais} and to score classroom discourse \citep{wang2023chatgpt}.
Best-practice guidance stresses validation against human labels \citep{pangakis2023automated,tornberg2024best}.
\citet{dunivin2025scaling} showed that chain-of-thought prompting can match human coders on some hermeneutic codes; we
recompute his reported agreement from his released predictions (\S\ref{sec:eq6}). Recent studies compare prompting strategies
\citep{rodrigueztriana2026one}, benchmark many models against expert adjudication \citep{marston2026humanitarian}, design
human--AI workflows for deductive coding \citep{li2025fallshort,matveyenko2025blended,yuan2026calico}, and add model
self-critique \citep{dunivin2026selfreflection}. \citet{zhao2025confidence} combine self-confidence with inter-model
diversity to route segments to review, auto-accepting 35\% at under 5\% error on their data; we ask whether such
thresholds hold on held-out data and across codebooks. \citet{liu2026agreement} show that agreement with human consensus is not the same as
coding quality: blind experts often prefer the model's code. Our adjudication finds the same (\S\ref{sec:eq6}).

\paragraph{Decision models.} Jev introduced the ``System One'' interface \citep{typesafe2026jev}. \citet{denney2026jev}
compare Jev with LLMs on seven political-science replications for accuracy, cost and calibration. \citet{li2026jevjudge}
use it as an accept-or-escalate judge, and \citet{rafe2026crash} convert crash narratives into probabilistic variables.
\citet{ibrahim2026decision} and TypedBench \citep{sharma2026typedbench} are closest to our work (\S\ref{sec:intro}). Other
recent studies show that short, natural-sounding context can flip decision models' answers \citep{xu2026jevout}, that
rejection thresholds fitted on one dataset fail on another \citep{lu2026candidate}, and that decisions can be read from an
ordinary LLM's next-token probabilities without retraining, with option-order bias reduced by averaging over rotations
\citep{li2026llmasjev,zhang2026anyjev}. We add the newer hosted and open models
\citep{openai2026decisions,cloudflare2026clef,perplexity2026decider,palmer2026kev}, qualitative codebooks with human
disagreement, threshold validity and guarantees that respect how hand-coded samples are drawn, and a comparison of related
9B systems.

\paragraph{Confidence, calibration and selective prediction.} Neural classifiers are often miscalibrated, and temperature
scaling \citep{guo2017calibration}, Platt scaling \citep{platt1999probabilistic} and isotonic regression
\citep{zadrozny2002transforming} are standard fixes. Whether confidence separates a model's correct from incorrect
predictions is a separate question \citep{hendrycks2017baseline}. For LLMs, confidence can be read from token
probabilities \citep{kadavath2022language} or elicited verbally \citep{tian2023just,xiong2024can}, and fast black-box
estimators are being developed \citep{hayes2026pinocchio}. Selective classification accepts a prediction only above a
confidence threshold \citep{geifman2017selective}, and cascades send the rest to a stronger model \citep{chen2024frugalgpt}.
For such thresholds, conformal risk control \citep{angelopoulos2024crc} bounds an expected monotone loss under
exchangeability, and standard Learn-then-Test \citep{angelopoulos2025ltt} gives a high-probability bound on population risk
from valid tests under assumptions such as i.i.d.\ calibration data; when observations come in groups, the
group becomes the unit of analysis \citep{dunn2023hierarchical}. For a fixed corpus, a finite-population betting test
\citep{waudbysmith2024betting} needs only that the hand-coded units, and the order they are visited in, are random; we adapt it
to coding decisions, and average over orders using e-values \citep{vovk2021evalues}. These guarantees have been applied to LLM judges
\citep{jung2025trust,badshah2026judge,li2026localize}, hybrid LLM--human annotation \citep{zeng2026hypac}, human
verification of LLM labels \citep{wang2024human} and calibrated grading \citep{raikote2026chillgrader}. We measure how often
the usual non-guaranteed rule breaks its target on real codebooks, show that a guarantee which ignores clustering fails, and
measure how much coverage a valid one costs at calibration sizes a research team can realistically hand-code.

\paragraph{Human label variation and downstream inference.} Annotators disagree for principled reasons, so a single
majority label is an imperfect target \citep{plank2022problem,uma2021learning}. \citet{baan2022stop} argue that
calibration should then be judged against the distribution of human labels, and \citet{lee2023dissenting} test whether LLMs
reproduce that distribution. Our RQ2 asks the triage version of this question: whether model uncertainty is high where
humans split. A complementary line of work does not accept individual labels at all. It corrects downstream estimates,
such as code prevalences or regression coefficients, using a hand-coded subsample
\citep{angelopoulos2023ppi,egami2023using}, and can use model confidence to choose which items to hand-code
\citep{gligoric2025unconfident}.

\paragraph{Contamination.} Public benchmarks may be in training data. We adapt the guided-versus-general completion test of
\citet{golchin2024time} and add a decoy-name control to separate memory from task framing.
